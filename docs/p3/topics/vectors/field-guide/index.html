Vector Notation

Read vectors as displacements with magnitude and direction.

What to notice

A vector describes movement in coordinate directions; a position vector starts at the origin.

Geometric Addition/Subtraction

Intersecting, Parallel, and Skew Lines

Classify two vector lines by direction and component checks.

What to notice

Direction vectors give the first test; simultaneous component equations decide whether non-parallel lines meet.

Example 1

Find an intersection

1. Try this first

Find the intersection of r=(120)+λ(2−11)\mathbf r=\begin{pmatrix}1\\2\\0\end{pmatrix}+\lambda\begin{pmatrix}2\\-1\\1\end{pmatrix}r=​120​​+λ​2−11​​ and r=(311)+μ(102)\mathbf r=\begin{pmatrix}3\\1\\1\end{pmatrix}+\mu\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\2\end{pmatrix}r=​311​​+μ​102​​.

2. First step

Rewrite direction scalar multiple as parallel or coincident. Stop before doing the full solution.

3. Hint

4. Idea

Principle: Line relationship test.

Show worked route

  2. Use x-components: 1+2λ=3+μ1+2\lambda=3+\mu1+2λ=3+μ, so μ=0\mu=0μ=0.
  3. Check z-components: λ=1+2μ\lambda=1+2\muλ=1+2μ, so 1=11=11=1.
  4. Substitute λ=1\lambda=1λ=1 into the first line to get (3,1,1)(3,1,1)(3,1,1).

Result: The lines intersect at (3,1,1)(3,1,1)(3,1,1).

5. Try similar

Classify two 3D lines after solving gives λ=2\lambda=2λ=2, μ=1\mu=1μ=1, but the z-component does not match.

  • Compare directions
  • Solve two components
  • Check third
  • Classify
Reveal/check the worked route
  1. If the directions are not scalar multiples, the lines are not parallel.
  2. The first two component equations may give candidate parameter values.
  3. If the third component fails with those same values, the lines do not meet.

6. Exam prep

Exam transfer: the question may hide this inside a+λb=c+μd \mathbf a+\lambda\mathbf b=\mathbf c+\mu\mathbf d a+λb=c+μd or phrase it as a request to classify two vector lines by direction and component checks.
