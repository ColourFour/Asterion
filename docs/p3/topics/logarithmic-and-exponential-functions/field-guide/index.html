Example 2

Write 2ln⁡x+ln⁡5−ln⁡(x−1)2\ln x+\ln 5-\ln(x-1)2lnx+ln5−ln(x−1) as a single logarithm, stating the domain.

  3. So the expression is ln⁡(5x2x−1)\ln\left(\frac{5x^2}{x-1}\right)ln(x−15x2​).

Section 1 of 6

Log Graphs and Inverses

Connect logarithmic graphs with reflected exponential graphs.

What to notice

A logarithm is the inverse operation for an exponential with the same base. The inverse graph swaps coordinates and reflects in $y=x$.

Example 1

Swap an exponential point

The point (3,8)(3,8)(3,8) lies on y=2xy=2^xy=2x. State the matching point on y=log⁡2xy=\log_2xy=log2​x and the graph meaning.

  1. The inverse of y=2xy=2^xy=2x is y=log⁡2xy=\log_2xy=log2​x.
  2. Inverse points swap coordinates.
  3. So (3,8)(3,8)(3,8) on the exponential graph becomes (8,3)(8,3)(8,3) on the logarithmic graph.
  4. The two graphs are reflections in the line y=xy=xy=x.

Result: (8,3) (8,3) (8,3)

Try a similar one

If (2,9)(2,9)(2,9) lies on y=3xy=3^xy=3x, state the matching point on y=log⁡3xy=\log_3xy=log3​x.

  • Name the inverse
  • Swap coordinates
  • State the log graph
  • Mention the reflection
Reveal/check the worked route
  1. y=log⁡3xy=\log_3xy=log3​x is the inverse of y=3xy=3^xy=3x.
  2. Inverse points swap their coordinates.
  3. So (2,9)(2,9)(2,9) becomes (9,2)(9,2)(9,2).

Try result: (9,2) (9,2) (9,2)

Section 2 of 6

Laws of Logarithms

Use product, quotient, and power laws without inventing false laws.

What to notice

Log laws work because logarithms record exponents. They apply to products, quotients, and powers, not to sums inside one logarithm.

Example 1

Combine only valid terms

Write 2ln⁡x+ln⁡5−ln⁡(x−1)2\ln x+\ln 5-\ln(x-1)2lnx+ln5−ln(x−1) as a single logarithm, stating the domain.

  1. Use the power law first: 2ln⁡x=ln⁡(x2)2\ln x=\ln(x^2)2lnx=ln(x2).
  2. Adding logs multiplies inputs and subtracting logs divides inputs.
  3. So the expression is ln⁡(5x2x−1)\ln\left(\frac{5x^2}{x-1}\right)ln(x−15x2​).
  4. The original domain needs x>0x>0x>0 and x−1>0x-1>0x−1>0, so x>1x>1x>1.

Result: ln⁡(5x2x−1),x>1 \ln\left(\frac{5x^2}{x-1}\right),\quad x>1 ln(x−15x2​),x>1

Try a similar one

Combine ln⁡(x+2)−3ln⁡x\ln(x+2)-3\ln xln(x+2)−3lnx into one logarithm and state the domain.

  • Move the power
  • Use quotient law
  • State original-domain restrictions
  • Do not split sums
Reveal/check the worked route
  1. 3ln⁡x=ln⁡(x3)3\ln x=\ln(x^3)3lnx=ln(x3).
  2. Then ln⁡(x+2)−ln⁡(x3)=ln⁡(x+2x3)\ln(x+2)-\ln(x^3)=\ln\left(\frac{x+2}{x^3}\right)ln(x+2)−ln(x3)=ln(x3x+2​).
  3. The original logs need x+2>0x+2>0x+2>0 and x>0x>0x>0, so x>0x>0x>0.

Try result: ln⁡(x+2x3),x>0 \ln\left(\frac{x+2}{x^3}\right),\quad x>0 ln(x3x+2​),x>0

log⁡a−log⁡b\log a-\log bloga−logb becomes log⁡(a/b)\log(a/b)log(a/b)
  • Move coefficients into powers before combining.
  • There is no law for splitting log⁡(a+b)\log(a+b)log(a+b).

Section 3 of 6

$e$ and Natural Logarithms

Use $e^x$ and $\ln x$ as inverse operations.

What to notice

Natural logarithms are logarithms to base $e$. Use $\ln$ after the exponential part is isolated.

Example 1

Isolate then use ln

Solve 5e3x=205e^{3x}=205e3x=20 exactly.

  1. Isolate the exponential term first: e3x=4e^{3x}=4e3x=4.
  2. Take natural logs of both sides: 3x=ln⁡43x=\ln43x=ln4.
  3. Divide by 333.
  4. So x=13ln⁡4x=\frac13\ln4x=31​ln4.

Result: x=13ln⁡4 x=\frac13\ln4 x=31​ln4

Try a similar one

Solve 2ex+1=92e^{x+1}=92ex+1=9 exactly.

  • Divide first
  • Take ln
  • Solve for x
  • Keep the answer exact
Reveal/check the worked route
  1. Divide by 222: ex+1=92e^{x+1}=\frac92ex+1=29​.
  2. Take natural logs: x+1=ln⁡(92)x+1=\ln\left(\frac92\right)x+1=ln(29​).
  3. Subtract 111.

Try result: x=ln⁡(92)−1 x=\ln\left(\frac92\right)-1 x=ln(29​)−1

Extra method table and checks
Natural-log inverse
eu=ke^u=keu=k take ln u=ln⁡ku=\ln ku=lnk
ln⁡(eu)\ln(e^u)ln(eu) simplifies to uuu
scale factor isolate before taking logs
  • ln⁡\lnln is the inverse function for exe^xex.
  • Isolate the exponential expression before applying ln⁡\lnln.

Section 4 of 6

Log Equations and Inequalities

Solve with log laws, then reject values outside the original domain.

What to notice

Every logarithm input must be positive. Algebraic roots from combined logs are only candidates until checked in the original equation.

Example 1

Reject the invalid root

Solve ln⁡(x−2)+ln⁡(x+1)=ln⁡10\ln(x-2)+\ln(x+1)=\ln10ln(x−2)+ln(x+1)=ln10.

  1. Domain first: x−2>0x-2>0x−2>0 and x+1>0x+1>0x+1>0, so x>2x>2x>2.
  2. Combine logs: ln⁡((x−2)(x+1))=ln⁡10\ln((x-2)(x+1))=\ln10ln((x−2)(x+1))=ln10.
  3. So (x−2)(x+1)=10(x-2)(x+1)=10(x−2)(x+1)=10, giving x=4x=4x=4 or x=−3x=-3x=−3.
  4. Reject x=−3x=-3x=−3 because it makes x−2x-2x−2 and x+1x+1x+1 negative in the original logs.
  5. Therefore x=4x=4x=4.

Result: x=4 x=4 x=4

Try a similar one

Solve ln⁡(2x)−ln⁡3=ln⁡4\ln(2x)-\ln3=\ln4ln(2x)−ln3=ln4, checking the domain.

  • State domain
  • Combine logs
  • Compare arguments
  • Reject if needed
Reveal/check the worked route
  1. The domain is 2x>02x>02x>0, so x>0x>0x>0.
  2. Combine: ln⁡(2x3)=ln⁡4\ln\left(\frac{2x}{3}\right)=\ln4ln(32x​)=ln4.
  3. Compare arguments: 2x3=4\frac{2x}{3}=432x​=4, so x=6x=6x=6, which satisfies the domain.

Try result: x=6 x=6 x=6

Section 5 of 6

Exponential Equations and Inequalities

Solve exponential equations and reverse inequalities when $0<a<1$.

What to notice

For equal positive bases, compare exponents. If the base is between 0 and 1, the function decreases, so inequality direction reverses.

Example 1

Decreasing-base reversal

Solve (12)x≤18\left(\frac12\right)^x\le\frac18(21​)x≤81​.

  1. Rewrite the right side with the same base: 18=(12)3\frac18=\left(\frac12\right)^381​=(21​)3.
  2. The base 12\frac1221​ is between 000 and 111, so the exponential function is decreasing.
  3. Because the function decreases, (12)x≤(12)3\left(\frac12\right)^x\le\left(\frac12\right)^3(21​)x≤(21​)3 gives x≥3x\ge3x≥3.

Result: x≥3 x\ge3 x≥3

Try a similar one

Solve 3x>273^x>273x>27, then solve (13)x>127\left(\frac13\right)^x>\frac1{27}(31​)x>271​.

  • Rewrite with matching bases
  • Decide increasing or decreasing
  • Compare exponents
  • Reverse only for decreasing base
Reveal/check the worked route
  1. 27=3327=3^327=33, so 3x>333^x>3^33x>33 gives x>3x>3x>3.
  2. 127=(13)3\frac1{27}=\left(\frac13\right)^3271​=(31​)3.
  3. The base 13\frac1331​ is decreasing, so (13)x>(13)3\left(\frac13\right)^x>\left(\frac13\right)^3(31​)x>(31​)3 gives x<3x<3x<3.

Try result: x>3,x<3 x>3,\quad x<3 x>3,x<3

Extra method table and checks

  • Matching bases lets you compare exponents.
  • A base between 000 and 111 reverses exponential inequalities.
  • Skill Check includes both increasing-base and decreasing-base inequalities.

Section 6 of 6

Log Linearisation

Turn a relationship into straight-line form and interpret axes.

What to notice

Taking logs can turn power or exponential relationships into $Y=c+mX$, where the chosen axes decide the gradient and intercept.

Example 1

Power law to line

For y=2x3y=2x^3y=2x3, write a straight-line form using logs.

  1. Take natural logs: ln⁡y=ln⁡(2x3)\ln y=\ln(2x^3)lny=ln(2x3).
  2. Split the product: ln⁡y=ln⁡2+ln⁡(x3)\ln y=\ln2+\ln(x^3)lny=ln2+ln(x3).
  3. Bring down the power: ln⁡y=ln⁡2+3ln⁡x\ln y=\ln2+3\ln xlny=ln2+3lnx.
  4. Plot ln⁡y\ln ylny against ln⁡x\ln xlnx for gradient 333 and intercept ln⁡2\ln2ln2.

Result: ln⁡y=ln⁡2+3ln⁡x \ln y=\ln2+3\ln x lny=ln2+3lnx

Try a similar one

Linearise y=5x2y=5x^2y=5x2 and state the gradient and intercept for a graph of ln⁡y\ln ylny against ln⁡x\ln xlnx.

  • Take logs
  • Split product
  • Move power
  • Name gradient and intercept
Reveal/check the worked route
  1. Take logs: ln⁡y=ln⁡(5x2)\ln y=\ln(5x^2)lny=ln(5x2).
  2. Split and move the power: ln⁡y=ln⁡5+2ln⁡x\ln y=\ln5+2\ln xlny=ln5+2lnx.
  3. For ln⁡y\ln ylny against ln⁡x\ln xlnx, the gradient is 222 and the intercept is ln⁡5\ln5ln5.

Try result: ln⁡y=ln⁡5+2ln⁡x,m=2,c=ln⁡5 \ln y=\ln5+2\ln x,\quad m=2,\quad c=\ln5 lny=ln5+2lnx,m=2,c=ln5

  • Gradient is the coefficient of the new horizontal variable.
  • Skill Check asks for gradient, intercept, and new-variable interpretation.
