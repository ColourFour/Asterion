Section 1 of 4

Cartesian and Conjugate

Work safely with complex numbers and their conjugates.

What to notice

The conjugate changes the sign of the imaginary part.

Support skill: this rehearses complex-number algebra before P3 modulus, argument, loci, and roots. It is not exam-exam practice by itself.

Example 1

Use the conjugate

Simplify (3+2i)(3−2i)(3+2i)(3-2i)(3+2i)(3−2i).

  1. The conjugate of 3+2i3+2i3+2i is 3−2i3-2i3−2i.
  2. Multiply: 9−6i+6i−4i29-6i+6i-4i^29−6i+6i−4i2.
  3. Since i2=−1i^2=-1i2=−1, this becomes 9+4=139+4=139+4=13.

Result: 13 13 13

  3. Since i2=−1i^2=-1i2=−1, this is 16+1=1716+1=1716+1=17.

Modulus and Argument

Locus

Read an Argand condition as a geometric path.

What to notice

A modulus condition is usually a distance statement.

Example 1

Circle from distance

Describe the locus ∣z−2∣=3|z-2|=3∣z−2∣=3.

  1. z−2z-2z−2 means distance from the point 2+0i2+0i2+0i.
  2. The distance is always 333.
  3. So the locus is a circle centre (2,0)(2,0)(2,0) radius 333.

Result: Circle centre (2,0)(2,0)(2,0), radius 333.

Try a similar one

Describe ∣z+1−i∣=2|z+1-i|=2∣z+1−i∣=2.

Reveal/check the worked route

  2. So the fixed point is −1+i-1+i−1+i, which has coordinates (−1,1)(-1,1)(−1,1).
  3. The distance from this point is always 222.

Try result: Circle centre (−1,1)(-1,1)(−1,1), radius 222.

Roots

Use modulus and argument to find complex roots.

What to notice

Roots share modulus changes and split arguments evenly.

Example 1

Cube roots of a real number

Find the arguments of the roots of z3=8z^3=8z3=8.

  1. Write 8=8ei2kπ8=8e^{i2k\pi}8=8ei2kπ.
  2. Cube-root the modulus: ∣z∣=2|z|=2∣z∣=2.
  3. Divide arguments by 333: θ=0,2π3,4π3\theta=0,\frac{2\pi}{3},\frac{4\pi}{3}θ=0,32π​,34π​.

Result: θ=0, 2π3, 4π3 \theta=0,\ \frac{2\pi}{3},\ \frac{4\pi}{3} θ=0, 32π​, 34π​
