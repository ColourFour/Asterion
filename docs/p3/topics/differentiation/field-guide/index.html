Section 1 of 8

Exponential and Logarithmic Derivatives

Differentiate e^(f(x)) and ln(f(x)) while keeping log-only skills in Logarithmic and Exponential Functions.

What to notice

The base rules are that e^x differentiates to e^x, e^(f(x)) picks up the inside derivative, and ln(f(x)) differentiates to f'(x)/f(x).

Chain rule appears here only as a supporting method inside exponential and logarithmic differentiation.

Example 1

Differentiate an exponential and a log

Differentiate y=4e3x−2+ln⁡(5x+1)y=4e^{3x-2}+\ln(5x+1)y=4e3x−2+ln(5x+1).

  1. e3x−2e^{3x-2}e3x−2 keeps its exponential shape and picks up the inside derivative 333.
  2. 4e3x−24e^{3x-2}4e3x−2 therefore differentiates to 12e3x−212e^{3x-2}12e3x−2.
  3. ln⁡(5x+1)\ln(5x+1)ln(5x+1) differentiates to 55x+1\frac{5}{5x+1}5x+15​.
  4. So dydx=12e3x−2+55x+1\frac{dy}{dx}=12e^{3x-2}+\frac{5}{5x+1}dxdy​=12e3x−2+5x+15​.

Result: dydx=12e3x−2+55x+1 \frac{dy}{dx}=12e^{3x-2}+\frac{5}{5x+1} dxdy​=12e3x−2+5x+15​

Product Rule

Differentiate products without dropping either derivative term.

What to notice

When two x-dependent factors are multiplied, set them as u and v, differentiate each factor, then add the two product-rule terms.

Example 1

Product with an exponential factor

Differentiate y=e2x(x3−3)y=e^{2x}(x^3-3)y=e2x(x3−3).

  1. Let u=e2xu=e^{2x}u=e2x and v=x3−3v=x^3-3v=x3−3.
  2. u′=2e2xu\prime=2e^{2x}u′=2e2x and v′=3x2v\prime=3x^2v′=3x2.
  3. Use uv′+vu′uv\prime+vu\primeuv′+vu′.
  4. dydx=e2x(3x2)+(x3−3)(2e2x)\frac{dy}{dx}=e^{2x}(3x^2)+(x^3-3)(2e^{2x})dxdy​=e2x(3x2)+(x3−3)(2e2x).
  5. Factor the common exponential: dydx=e2x(2x3+3x2−6)\frac{dy}{dx}=e^{2x}(2x^3+3x^2-6)dxdy​=e2x(2x3+3x2−6).

Result: dydx=e2x(2x3+3x2−6) \frac{dy}{dx}=e^{2x}(2x^3+3x^2-6) dxdy​=e2x(2x3+3x2−6)

Quotient Rule

Differentiate one x-dependent expression divided by another.

What to notice

For y = u/v, identify u and v clearly, differentiate both, then keep the numerator order as v u' minus u v'.

Example 1

Quotient with a polynomial denominator

Differentiate y=x3x2+1y=\frac{x^3}{x^2+1}y=x2+1x3​.

  1. Let u=x3u=x^3u=x3 and v=x2+1v=x^2+1v=x2+1.
  2. u′=3x2u\prime=3x^2u′=3x2 and v′=2xv\prime=2xv′=2x.
  3. Use vu′−uv′v2\frac{vu\prime-uv\prime}{v^2}v2vu′−uv′​.
  4. dydx=(x2+1)(3x2)−x3(2x)(x2+1)2\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{(x^2+1)(3x^2)-x^3(2x)}{(x^2+1)^2}dxdy​=(x2+1)2(x2+1)(3x2)−x3(2x)​.
  5. Simplify after the structure is correct: dydx=x4+3x2(x2+1)2\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{x^4+3x^2}{(x^2+1)^2}dxdy​=(x2+1)2x4+3x2​.

Result: dydx=x4+3x2(x2+1)2 \frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{x^4+3x^2}{(x^2+1)^2} dxdy​=(x2+1)2x4+3x2​

  3. Use vu′−uv′v2\frac{vu\prime-uv\prime}{v^2}v2vu′−uv′​.

Try result: (x2+1)−2x(x+2)(x2+1)2 \frac{(x^2+1)-2x(x+2)}{(x^2+1)^2} (x2+1)2(x2+1)−2x(x+2)​

Stationary Points, Tangents, and Normals

Turn a derivative into the gradient information the question asks for.

What to notice

These questions are not finished when dy/dx is found. Differentiate, substitute the point or solve dy/dx = 0, interpret the gradient, then form the requested line equation.

This bridge connects the reviewed stationary, tangent, and normal practice IDs for Differentiation without changing Exam Training selection.

Example 1

Find tangent and normal equations

For y=x2+1y=x^2+1y=x2+1 at x=3x=3x=3, find the tangent and normal equations, and state whether the point is stationary.

  1. Differentiate first: dydx=2x\frac{dy}{dx}=2xdxdy​=2x.
  2. At x=3x=3x=3, the point is (3,10)(3,10)(3,10) and the tangent gradient is m=6m=6m=6.
  3. Since m≠0m\ne0m=0, the point is not stationary.
  4. The tangent is y−10=6(x−3)y-10=6(x-3)y−10=6(x−3).
  5. The normal gradient is the negative reciprocal, mn=−16m_n=-\frac16mn​=−61​, so the normal is y−10=−16(x−3)y-10=-\frac16(x-3)y−10=−61​(x−3).

Result: tangent: y−10=6(x−3),normal: y−10=−16(x−3) \text{tangent: }y-10=6(x-3),\quad \text{normal: }y-10=-\frac16(x-3) tangent: y−10=6(x−3),normal: y−10=−61​(x−3)

sin/cos/tan Derivatives

Differentiate one trig expression by applying the base derivative and one chain-rule step.

What to notice

Use one base derivative, then multiply by the inside derivative. Keep trig identity work separate unless the derivative step needs it.

Trig simplification and equation solving remain in Trigonometry unless the assessed move is differentiation.

Differentiate a composite sine

Result: dydx=30xcos⁡(3x2) \frac{dy}{dx}=30x\cos(3x^2) dxdy​=30xcos(3x2)

Inverse Tangent Derivative

Recognize the inverse-tangent derivative form and apply one chain-rule step.

What to notice

Treat $\tan^{-1}(u)$ as inverse tangent, not as a reciprocal tangent expression.

This is narrow support for P3 differentiation follow-through when inverse tangent appears.

Implicit Differentiation

Differentiate equations where y is a function of x but is not isolated.

What to notice

Differentiate every term with respect to x. Whenever a y term is differentiated, multiply by dy/dx, then collect and solve for dy/dx.

Example 1

Solve for dy/dx

For x2−3y3=4y2x^2-3y^3=4y^2x2−3y3=4y2, find dydx\frac{dy}{dx}dxdy​.

  4. So dydx=2x9y2+8y\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{2x}{9y^2+8y}dxdy​=9y2+8y2x​.

Result: dydx=2x9y2+8y \frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{2x}{9y^2+8y} dxdy​=9y2+8y2x​

Parametric Differentiation

Find dy/dx from x and y written in terms of a parameter.

What to notice

Differentiate x and y separately with respect to the same parameter, divide dy/dt by dx/dt, then substitute a parameter value only after the derivative formula is found.

Example 1

Use derivative ratio

If x=2t3x=2t^3x=2t3 and y=5t2−6y=5t^2-6y=5t2−6, find dydx\frac{dy}{dx}dxdy​ in terms of t.

  4. So dydx=53t\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{5}{3t}dxdy​=3t5​ when t≠0t\ne0t=0.

Result: dydx=53t \frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{5}{3t} dxdy​=3t5​
