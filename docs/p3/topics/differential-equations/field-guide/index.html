First-Order Differential Equation Idea

Translate a rate statement into a first-order differential equation before solving anything.

What to notice

A differential equation connects a changing quantity with its rate of change. In first-order models, the derivative might be dy/dx, dh/dt, dT/dt, or dx/dt.

Example 1

Build the rate equation

1. Try this first

Water enters a tank at 444 cm/min. The depth hhh leaks out at a rate proportional to h\sqrt hh​. Write a differential equation for hhh.

2. First step

Rewrite rate of h as dh/dtdh/dtdh/dt. Stop before doing the full solution.

3. Hint

Changing quantity: depth hhh. Independent variable: time ttt.

4. Idea

Principle: Words to symbols.

Show worked route
  1. Changing quantity: depth hhh. Independent variable: time ttt.
  2. Inflow increases depth, so it contributes +4+4+4.
  3. Leak rate is proportional to h\sqrt hh​, so it contributes −kh-k\sqrt h−kh​ for a positive constant kkk.
  4. The model is dhdt=4−kh\frac{dh}{dt}=4-k\sqrt hdtdh​=4−kh​.

Result: dhdt=4−kh \frac{dh}{dt}=4-k\sqrt h dtdh​=4−kh​

5. Try similar

A chemical amount xxx forms at a rate proportional to the amount still unformed from a maximum of 404040. Write a model.

  • Changing quantity
  • Rate derivative
  • Proportional quantity
  • Equation only
Reveal/check the worked route
  1. The changing quantity is xxx and the independent variable can be time ttt.
  2. The amount still unformed is 40−x40-x40−x.
  3. Use a positive proportionality constant kkk.

Try result: dxdt=k(40−x),k>0 \frac{dx}{dt}=k(40-x),\quad k>0 dtdx​=k(40−x),k>0

6. Exam prep

Exam transfer: the question may hide this inside dhdt=4−kh \frac{dh}{dt}=4-k\sqrt h dtdh​=4−kh​ or phrase it as a request to translate a rate statement into a first-order differential equation before solving anything. Recognize rate of h; your first move is to means before completing the solution.

  • Rate of change usually means a derivative such as dy/dxdy/dxdy/dx, dh/dtdh/dtdh/dt, or dT/dtdT/dtdT/dt.
  • Proportional to means multiply by a constant such as kkk.
  • Skill Check starts with forming the differential equation, not solving it.

Separable Variables Solutions

Rearrange a separable differential equation, integrate both sides, and state a general solution.

What to notice

A separable differential equation can be rearranged so all y terms sit with dy and all x terms sit with dx. Integration methods from Integration may appear after that separation step.

Example 1

General exponential family

1. Try this first

Solve dydx=2xy\frac{dy}{dx}=2xydxdy​=2xy for the general solution.

2. First step

Identify y terms, then with toward dydydy. Stop before doing the full solution.

3. Hint

Separate first: 1y dy=2x dx\frac{1}{y}\,dy=2x\,dxy1​dy=2xdx.

4. Idea

Principle: Variable sides.

Show worked route
  1. Separate first: 1y dy=2x dx\frac{1}{y}\,dy=2x\,dxy1​dy=2xdx.
  2. Integrate both sides: ln⁡∣y∣=x2+c\ln|y|=x^2+cln∣y∣=x2+c.
  3. Exponentiate: ∣y∣=ecex2|y|=e^c e^{x^2}∣y∣=ecex2.
  4. Absorb the sign and ece^cec into one constant: y=Aex2y=Ae^{x^2}y=Aex2.

Result: y=Aex2 y=Ae^{x^2} y=Aex2

5. Try similar

Solve dydx=3y\frac{dy}{dx}=3ydxdy​=3y and give the general solution.

  • Separate
  • Integrate both sides
  • Add constant
  • Absorb constant if exponentiating
Reveal/check the worked route
  1. Separate: 1y dy=3 dx\frac1y\,dy=3\,dxy1​dy=3dx.
  2. Integrate: ln⁡∣y∣=3x+c\ln|y|=3x+cln∣y∣=3x+c.
  3. Exponentiate and absorb the constant.

Try result: y=Ae3x y=Ae^{3x} y=Ae3x

6. Exam prep

Exam transfer: the question may hide this inside dydx=xy \frac{dy}{dx}=xy dxdy​=xy or phrase it as a request to rearrange a separable differential equation, integrate both sides, and state a general solution. Recognize y terms; your first move is to with before completing the solution.

Extra method table and checks
Variable sides

x terms with dxdxdx
constant absorbs into AAA when exponentiating
  • Separate before integrating.
  • A solution may remain implicit unless the question asks for yyy explicitly.
  • Skill Check includes general solutions, trig forms, log/partial-fraction forms, and a stretch by-parts-after-separation item.

Finding Particular Solutions

Use an initial condition or point on the curve to find the arbitrary constant.

What to notice

First find the general solution. Then substitute the given point or initial condition, solve for the constant, and write the particular solution.

Example 1

Use the point after solving

1. Try this first

Find the particular solution of dydx=2y\frac{dy}{dx}=2ydxdy​=2y passing through (0,3)(0,3)(0,3).

2. First step

Identify general solution, then then toward condition. Stop before doing the full solution.

3. Hint

General solution: y=Ae2xy=Ae^{2x}y=Ae2x.

4. Idea

Principle: Condition step.

Show worked route
  1. General solution: y=Ae2xy=Ae^{2x}y=Ae2x.
  2. The point (0,3)(0,3)(0,3) means x=0x=0x=0 and y=3y=3y=3.
  3. Substitute: 3=Ae03=Ae^03=Ae0, so A=3A=3A=3.
  4. Replace the constant in the solution.

Result: y=3e2x y=3e^{2x} y=3e2x

5. Try similar

The general solution is y=A(x2+3)2y=A(x^2+3)^2y=A(x2+3)2. If y=2y=2y=2 when x=0x=0x=0, find AAA and write the particular solution.

  • Start from the solution
  • Substitute both coordinates
  • Solve for the constant
  • Rewrite without unknown C or A
Reveal/check the worked route
  1. Substitute x=0x=0x=0 and y=2y=2y=2: 2=A(02+3)22=A(0^2+3)^22=A(02+3)2.
  2. So 2=9A2=9A2=9A, giving A=29A=\frac29A=92​.
  3. Put this value back into the solution.

Try result: A=29,y=29(x2+3)2 A=\frac29,\quad y=\frac29(x^2+3)^2 A=92​,y=92​(x2+3)2

6. Exam prep

Exam transfer: the question may hide this inside dydx=2y, y(0)=3 \frac{dy}{dx}=2y,\ y(0)=3 dxdy​=2y, y(0)=3 or phrase it as a request to use an initial condition or point on the curve to find the arbitrary constant. Recognize general solution; your first move is to then before completing the solution.

Modeling With Differential Equations

Form, solve, and interpret a first-order differential equation from a context.

What to notice

Modeling questions combine the setup step with separation, constants from data, and a final contextual answer.

Example 1

Cooling model with a constant

1. Try this first

A cup has temperature TTT degrees C in a room at 202020 degrees C. It cools at a rate proportional to T−20T-20T−20, and T=80T=80T=80 when t=0t=0t=0. Form and solve the model.

2. First step

Identify changing quantity, then choose toward TTT against ttt. Stop before doing the full solution.

3. Hint

Cooling means the derivative is negative: dTdt=−k(T−20)\frac{dT}{dt}=-k(T-20)dtdT​=−k(T−20), where k>0k>0k>0.

4. Idea

Principle: Context to answer.

Show worked route
  1. Cooling means the derivative is negative: dTdt=−k(T−20)\frac{dT}{dt}=-k(T-20)dtdT​=−k(T−20), where k>0k>0k>0.
  2. Separate: 1T−20 dT=−k dt\frac{1}{T-20}\,dT=-k\,dtT−201​dT=−kdt.
  3. Integrate: ln⁡∣T−20∣=−kt+c\ln|T-20|=-kt+cln∣T−20∣=−kt+c.
  4. So T−20=Ae−ktT-20=Ae^{-kt}T−20=Ae−kt. Use T(0)=80T(0)=80T(0)=80 to get A=60A=60A=60.

Result: T=20+60e−kt T=20+60e^{-kt} T=20+60e−kt

5. Try similar

A reaction forms amount xxx toward a maximum of 505050 at rate 2(50−x)2(50-x)2(50−x). Given x=0x=0x=0 when t=0t=0t=0, solve for xxx.

  • Form dx/dt
  • Separate
  • Integrate
  • Use initial condition
Reveal/check the worked route
  1. dxdt=2(50−x)\frac{dx}{dt}=2(50-x)dtdx​=2(50−x).
  2. Separate: 150−x dx=2 dt\frac1{50-x}\,dx=2\,dt50−x1​dx=2dt.
  3. Integrate: −ln⁡∣50−x∣=2t+c-\ln|50-x|=2t+c−ln∣50−x∣=2t+c, so 50−x=Ae−2t50-x=Ae^{-2t}50−x=Ae−2t.
  4. Use x(0)=0x(0)=0x(0)=0 to get A=50A=50A=50.

Try result: x=50−50e−2t x=50-50e^{-2t} x=50−50e−2t

6. Exam prep

Exam transfer: the question may hide this inside dTdt=−k(T−20) \frac{dT}{dt}=-k(T-20) dtdT​=−k(T−20) or phrase it as a request to form, solve, and interpret a first-order differential equation from a context. Recognize changing quantity; your first move is to choose before completing the solution.
