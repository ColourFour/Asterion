Section 1 of 5

Secant, Cosecant, and Cotangent

Treat reciprocal trig functions as sine, cosine, and tangent rewrites before solving.

What to notice

Secant, cosecant, and cotangent are reciprocal functions. Their graphs inherit vertical asymptotes where the denominator function is zero.

Support skill: reciprocal functions prepare safe P3 trig transformations. Exam readiness still comes from reviewed P3 exam-image attempts.

Example 1

Solve a reciprocal equation

Solve sec⁡x=2\sec x=2secx=2 for 0≤x<2π0\le x<2\pi0≤x<2π and state the graph restriction.

  1. Use the definition sec⁡x=1cos⁡x\sec x=\frac1{\cos x}secx=cosx1​, so 1cos⁡x=2\frac1{\cos x}=2cosx1​=2.
  2. This gives cos⁡x=12\cos x=\frac12cosx=21​.
  3. Cosine is positive in quadrants I and IV, so x=π3x=\frac{\pi}{3}x=3π​ or x=5π3x=\frac{5\pi}{3}x=35π​.
  4. The graph of y=sec⁡xy=\sec xy=secx has vertical asymptotes where cos⁡x=0\cos x=0cosx=0, such as x=π2x=\frac{\pi}{2}x=2π​ and x=3π2x=\frac{3\pi}{2}x=23π​ in this interval.

Result: x=π3, 5π3 x=\frac{\pi}{3},\ \frac{5\pi}{3} x=3π​, 35π​

Expanded Pythagorean Identities

Use reciprocal Pythagorean identities for proofs and equations.

What to notice

The base identity $\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1$ creates two expanded forms by dividing by $\cos^2\theta$ or by $\sin^2\theta$.

Support skill: this replaces the old identity-rewrite topic with the approved expanded identity plan.

Example 1

Prove then use the identity

Show why 1+tan⁡2θ=sec⁡2θ1+\tan^2\theta=\sec^2\theta1+tan2θ=sec2θ, then solve 1+tan⁡2θ=41+\tan^2\theta=41+tan2θ=4 for 0≤θ<π0\le\theta<\pi0≤θ<π.

  1. Start with sin⁡2θ+cos⁡2θ=1\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1sin2θ+cos2θ=1.
  2. Divide every term by cos⁡2θ\cos^2\thetacos2θ to get tan⁡2θ+1=sec⁡2θ\tan^2\theta+1=\sec^2\thetatan2θ+1=sec2θ.
  3. For the equation, 1+tan⁡2θ=41+\tan^2\theta=41+tan2θ=4 gives tan⁡2θ=3\tan^2\theta=3tan2θ=3.
  4. So tan⁡θ=±3\tan\theta=\pm\sqrt3tanθ=±3​, giving θ=π3\theta=\frac{\pi}{3}θ=3π​ or θ=2π3\theta=\frac{2\pi}{3}θ=32π​ in 0≤θ<π0\le\theta<\pi0≤θ<π.

Result: θ=π3, 2π3 \theta=\frac{\pi}{3},\ \frac{2\pi}{3} θ=3π​, 32π​

Try a similar one

Use 1+cot⁡2θ=cosec⁡2θ1+\cot^2\theta=\operatorname{cosec}^2\theta1+cot2θ=cosec2θ to solve cosec⁡2θ=2\operatorname{cosec}^2\theta=2cosec2θ=2 for 0≤θ<π0\le\theta<\pi0≤θ<π.

  • Solve the squared trig equation

Reveal/check the worked route
  1. 1+cot⁡2θ=21+\cot^2\theta=21+cot2θ=2, so cot⁡2θ=1\cot^2\theta=1cot2θ=1.
  2. Hence cot⁡θ=±1\cot\theta=\pm1cotθ=±1, so tan⁡θ=±1\tan\theta=\pm1tanθ=±1.
  3. In 0≤θ<π0\le\theta<\pi0≤θ<π, the solutions are θ=π4\theta=\frac{\pi}{4}θ=4π​ and θ=3π4\theta=\frac{3\pi}{4}θ=43π​.

Try result: θ=π4, 3π4 \theta=\frac{\pi}{4},\ \frac{3\pi}{4} θ=4π​, 43π​

Trigonometric Addition Formulae

Use compound-angle formulae for exact values and shifted-angle equations.

What to notice

Addition formulae turn a compound angle into known sine, cosine, or tangent values. For shifted equations, solve the compound angle first, then shift back into the required interval.

Example 1

Exact value and shifted equation

Find sin⁡75∘\sin75^\circsin75∘, then solve sin⁡(x+π6)=12\sin\left(x+\frac{\pi}{6}\right)=\frac12sin(x+6π​)=21​ for 0≤x<2π0\le x<2\pi0≤x<2π.

Trigonometric Double-Angle Formulae

Pick the double-angle form that matches the expression or equation.

What to notice

Double-angle formulae are useful when one angle is twice another, or when a squared trig term needs to become a doubled angle.

Example 1

Choose the sine form

Rewrite 1−cos⁡2x1-\cos2x1−cos2x using sin⁡x\sin xsinx, then explain how interval solving avoids lost answers.

  1. Use cos⁡2x=1−2sin⁡2x\cos2x=1-2\sin^2xcos2x=1−2sin2x.
  2. Substitute: 1−cos⁡2x=1−(1−2sin⁡2x)1-\cos2x=1-(1-2\sin^2x)1−cos2x=1−(1−2sin2x).
  3. Simplify the brackets to get 2sin⁡2x2\sin^2x2sin2x.
  4. When a double-angle equation factors, solve each factor over the interval instead of dividing by a trig factor that may be zero.

Result: 2sin⁡2x 2\sin^2x 2sin2x

R-Form Transformations

Rewrite a sine/cosine sum as one shifted trig function.

What to notice

R-form transformations combine $a\sin x+b\cos x$ into one shifted wave. Match coefficients after expanding the chosen sine or cosine form.

Example 1

Find R and alpha

Write 3sin⁡x+4cos⁡x3\sin x+4\cos x3sinx+4cosx as Rsin⁡(x+α)R\sin(x+\alpha)Rsin(x+α).

  1. Expand: Rsin⁡(x+α)=Rsin⁡xcos⁡α+Rcos⁡xsin⁡αR\sin(x+\alpha)=R\sin x\cos\alpha+R\cos x\sin\alphaRsin(x+α)=Rsinxcosα+Rcosxsinα.
  2. Match coefficients: Rcos⁡α=3R\cos\alpha=3Rcosα=3 and Rsin⁡α=4R\sin\alpha=4Rsinα=4.
  3. So R=32+42=5R=\sqrt{3^2+4^2}=5R=32+42​=5.
  4. tan⁡α=43\tan\alpha=\frac43tanα=34​.

Result: 5sin⁡(x+α),tan⁡α=43 5\sin(x+\alpha),\quad \tan\alpha=\frac43 5sin(x+α),tanα=34​
