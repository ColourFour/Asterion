Skill 1 of 11

Form a straight-line equation

Build a line equation from a point and gradient.

Need to know

  • Use y−y1=m(x−x1)y-y_1=m(x-x_1)y−y1​=m(x−x1​).
  • Rearrange only after substituting the known point and gradient.

Common error: Substituting the point with reversed signs.

Worked example

Find the line through (2,3)(2,3)(2,3) with gradient 4.

  1. y−3=4(x−2)y-3=4(x-2)y−3=4(x−2).
  2. Expand to get y=4x−5y=4x-5y=4x−5.

Answer: y=4x−5y=4x-5y=4x−5

Skill 2 of 11

Find a midpoint from two coordinates

Use coordinate formulae and gradient relationships.

Need to know

  • Average the x-coordinates.
  • Average the y-coordinates separately.

Common error: Adding coordinates but forgetting to divide each total by 2.

Worked example

Find the midpoint of (−2,5)(-2,5)(−2,5) and (4,1)(4,1)(4,1).

  1. The x-coordinate is (−2+4)/2=1(-2+4)/2=1(−2+4)/2=1.
  2. The y-coordinate is (5+1)/2=3(5+1)/2=3(5+1)/2=3.

Answer: (1,3)(1,3)(1,3)

Skill 3 of 11

Find the distance between two coordinates

Calculate a coordinate distance exactly.

Need to know

  • Find the horizontal and vertical differences.
  • Apply Pythagoras and simplify the resulting square root.

Common error: Adding coordinate differences without squaring them.

Worked example

Find the distance between (1,2)(1,2)(1,2) and (4,6)(4,6)(4,6).

  1. The differences are 3 and 4.
  2. The distance is 32+42=5\sqrt{3^2+4^2}=532+42​=5.

Answer: 5

Skill 4 of 11

Use parallel and perpendicular gradients

Choose a gradient from a parallel or perpendicular condition.

Need to know

  • Parallel lines share a gradient.
  • Perpendicular non-vertical gradients are negative reciprocals.

Common error: Negating a gradient without taking its reciprocal.

Worked example

Find the gradient perpendicular to a line of gradient 3.

  1. Let the new gradient be m.
  2. 3m=−13m=-13m=−1.

Answer: m=−1/3m=-1/3m=−1/3

Skill 5 of 11

Find the intersection of two straight lines

Solve two line equations for their shared point.

Need to know

  • Equate expressions for the same coordinate.
  • Substitute the result back to obtain the other coordinate.

Common error: Reporting only the x-coordinate.

Worked example

Find where y=2x+1y=2x+1y=2x+1 meets y=7−xy=7-xy=7−x.

  1. 2x+1=7−x2x+1=7-x2x+1=7−x gives x=2x=2x=2.
  2. Then y=5y=5y=5.

Answer: (2,5)(2,5)(2,5)

Skill 6 of 11

Convert an expanded circle equation to centre-radius form

Read a circle centre and radius from its equation.

Need to know

  • In (x−a)2+(y−b)2=r2(x-a)^2+(y-b)^2=r^2(x−a)2+(y−b)2=r2, the centre is (a,b)(a,b)(a,b).
  • Complete both squares when converting an expanded equation.

Common error: Reading the signs inside the brackets without reversing them.

Worked example

Find the centre and radius of (x−2)2+(y+1)2=16(x-2)^2+(y+1)^2=16(x−2)2+(y+1)2=16.

  1. The bracket shifts give centre (2,−1)(2,-1)(2,−1).
  2. r2=16r^2=16r2=16, so r=4r=4r=4.

Answer: Centre (2,−1)(2,-1)(2,−1), radius 4

Skill 7 of 11

Form a circle equation from its centre and radius

Write a circle equation from centre and radius data.

Need to know

  • Use (x−a)2+(y−b)2=r2(x-a)^2+(y-b)^2=r^2(x−a)2+(y−b)2=r2.
  • Reverse neither centre coordinate when substituting into a and b.

Common error: Writing r rather than r2r^2r2 on the right.

Worked example

Form the circle with centre (2,−3)(2,-3)(2,−3) and radius 4.

  1. The brackets are (x−2)(x-2)(x−2) and (y+3)(y+3)(y+3).
  2. r2=16r^2=16r2=16.

Answer: (x−2)2+(y+3)2=16(x-2)^2+(y+3)^2=16(x−2)2+(y+3)2=16

Skill 8 of 11

Recognise an algebraic tangency condition

Recognise tangency through a repeated algebraic intersection.

Need to know

  • Substitute the line into the circle.
  • A tangent produces one repeated root, so the resulting discriminant is zero.

Common error: Treating a tangent as two distinct intersections.

Worked example

What discriminant condition shows that a line is tangent to a circle?

  1. A tangent meets the circle at one repeated point.
  2. A repeated quadratic root has discriminant zero.

Answer: D=0D=0D=0

Skill 9 of 11

Use the perpendicular radius to find a tangent gradient

Use radius geometry to find a tangent gradient.

Need to know

  • Find the radius gradient from the centre to the contact point.
  • Take its negative reciprocal for the tangent.

Common error: Using the radius gradient unchanged.

Worked example

A radius to a contact point has gradient 2. Find the tangent gradient.

  1. Radius and tangent are perpendicular.
  2. 2m=−12m=-12m=−1.

Answer: m=−1/2m=-1/2m=−1/2

Skill 10 of 11

Find line-and-circle intersection coordinates

Find every coordinate where a line meets a circle.

Need to know

  • Substitute the line into the circle to get one quadratic.
  • Back-substitute each root into the line.

Common error: Giving x-roots without their paired y-coordinates.

Worked example

Find where y=0y=0y=0 meets x2+y2=4x^2+y^2=4x2+y2=4.

  1. x2=4x^2=4x2=4, so x=±2x=\pm2x=±2.
  2. In both cases y=0.

Answer: (−2,0)(-2,0)(−2,0) and (2,0)(2,0)(2,0)

Skill 11 of 11

Connect graph intersections and equation roots

Translate graph contact into a root condition.

Need to know

  • Equate graph equations to form one equation.
  • Use the discriminant to distinguish crossing, touching and missing.

Common error: Applying the discriminant before reducing to one quadratic variable.

Worked example

What discriminant condition means a line touches a quadratic curve?

  1. Touching produces one shared point.
  2. The resulting quadratic has one repeated root.

Answer: D=0D=0D=0
