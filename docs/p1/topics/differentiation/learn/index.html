Skill 1 of 11

Interpret first- and second-derivative notation

Interpret first and second derivatives as information about a curve.

Need to know

  • The first derivative gives instantaneous gradient.
  • The second derivative describes how the gradient changes.

Common error: Treating the second derivative as the square of the first derivative.

Worked example

What does f′(2)=5f^{\prime}(2)=5f′(2)=5 say geometrically?

  1. The derivative is the tangent gradient.
  2. At x=2, that gradient is 5.

Answer: The tangent at x=2 has gradient 5

Skill 2 of 11

Interpret a derivative as a limiting gradient

Recognise a derivative as a limit of chord gradients.

Need to know

  • Use a second point separated by a small increment h.
  • Let h approach zero only after simplifying the difference quotient.

Common error: Substituting h=0 before simplifying.

Worked example

Form the difference quotient for f(x)=x2f(x)=x^2f(x)=x2.

  1. Use [(x+h)2−x2]/h[(x+h)^2-x^2]/h[(x+h)2−x2]/h.
  2. Simplify to 2x+h2x+h2x+h, then let h→0h\to0h→0.

Answer: f′(x)=2xf^{\prime}(x)=2xf′(x)=2x

Skill 3 of 11

Differentiate rational powers and linear combinations

Differentiate powers and simple composites accurately.

Need to know

  • For xnx^nxn, multiply by n and reduce the power by one.
  • For a composite, multiply by the inner derivative.

Common error: Applying the outer derivative but omitting the inner derivative.

Worked example

Differentiate 5x3/2−2x−15x^{3/2}-2x^{-1}5x3/2−2x−1.

  1. The first term gives 152x1/2\frac{15}{2}x^{1/2}215​x1/2.
  2. The second term gives +2x−2+2x^{-2}+2x−2.

Answer: 152x1/2+2x−2\frac{15}{2}x^{1/2}+2x^{-2}215​x1/2+2x−2

Skill 4 of 11

Differentiate simple composites with the chain rule

Differentiate a power of a linear expression.

Need to know

  • Differentiate the outer power.
  • Multiply by the derivative of the inner expression.

Common error: Omitting the constant inner derivative.

Worked example

Differentiate (3x+1)4(3x+1)^4(3x+1)4.

  1. The outer derivative is 4(3x+1)34(3x+1)^34(3x+1)3.
  2. Multiply by 3.

Answer: 12(3x+1)312(3x+1)^312(3x+1)3

Skill 5 of 11

Find a normal gradient and equation

Turn a derivative value into a line equation.

Need to know

  • Evaluate the derivative at the stated point.
  • For the normal, use the negative reciprocal gradient.

Common error: Finding the gradient but not writing the requested equation.

Worked example

Find the normal gradient to y=x2y=x^2y=x2 at x=2.

  1. dy/dx=2xdy/dx=2xdy/dx=2x, so the tangent gradient is 4.
  2. The normal gradient is the negative reciprocal.

Answer: −1/4-1/4−1/4

Skill 6 of 11

Find a tangent equation

Turn a derivative value into a tangent equation.

Need to know

  • Evaluate the derivative at the point.
  • Use point-gradient form and complete the requested rearrangement.

Common error: Stopping after finding the gradient.

Worked example

Find the tangent to y=x2y=x^2y=x2 at (2,4)(2,4)(2,4).

  1. dy/dx=2xdy/dx=2xdy/dx=2x, so the gradient is 4.
  2. y−4=4(x−2)y-4=4(x-2)y−4=4(x−2).

Answer: y=4x−4y=4x-4y=4x−4

Skill 7 of 11

Use derivative signs for increasing and decreasing intervals

Use derivative signs to describe curve direction.

Need to know

  • f′(x)>0f^{\prime}(x)>0f′(x)>0 means increasing.
  • f′(x)<0f^{\prime}(x)<0f′(x)<0 means decreasing.

Common error: Using the sign of f(x) instead of the sign of its derivative.

Worked example

For f′(x)=2x−4f^{\prime}(x)=2x-4f′(x)=2x−4, where is f increasing?

  1. Solve 2x−4>02x-4>02x−4>0.
  2. This gives x>2x>2x>2.

Answer: x>2x>2x>2

Skill 8 of 11

Solve connected rates-of-change problems

Link two changing quantities through one differentiated relation.

Need to know

  • Differentiate the geometric or algebraic relation with respect to time.
  • Keep units attached to the final rate.

Common error: Substituting values before differentiating and turning a variable into a constant.

Worked example

A circle radius grows at 2 cm/s. Find dA/dtdA/dtdA/dt when r=3 cm.

  1. A=πr2A=\pi r^2A=πr2 gives dA/dt=2πr dr/dtdA/dt=2\pi r\,dr/dtdA/dt=2πrdr/dt.
  2. Substitute r=3 and dr/dt=2dr/dt=2dr/dt=2.

Answer: 12π cm2/s12\pi\text{ cm}^2/\text{s}12π cm2/s

Skill 9 of 11

Classify a stationary point

Locate zero-gradient points and classify their nature.

Need to know

  • Solve f′(x)=0f^{\prime}(x)=0f′(x)=0.
  • A positive second derivative gives a local minimum; negative gives a local maximum.

Common error: Setting the second derivative to zero to locate every stationary point.

Worked example

Classify a stationary point where f′(a)=0f^{\prime}(a)=0f′(a)=0 and f′′(a)=2f^{\prime\prime}(a)=2f′′(a)=2.

  1. The zero first derivative confirms a stationary point.
  2. The positive second derivative gives a local minimum.

Answer: Local minimum

Skill 10 of 11

Locate stationary points

Find complete stationary-point coordinates.

Need to know

  • Solve f′(x)=0f^{\prime}(x)=0f′(x)=0.
  • Substitute every solution into the original function.

Common error: Reporting stationary x-values without y-coordinates.

Worked example

Locate the stationary point of f(x)=x2−6x+4f(x)=x^2-6x+4f(x)=x2−6x+4.

  1. f′(x)=2x−6=0f^{\prime}(x)=2x-6=0f′(x)=2x−6=0, so x=3x=3x=3.
  2. f(3)=9−18+4=−5f(3)=9-18+4=-5f(3)=9−18+4=−5.

Answer: (3,−5)(3,-5)(3,−5)

Skill 11 of 11

Sketch a curve using derivative information

Combine intercept and derivative information into a curve sketch.

Need to know

  • Plot known coordinates and stationary points first.
  • Join them using the correct increasing/decreasing behaviour and end shape.

Common error: Labelling an unsupported point of inflexion or omitting coordinate scale.

Worked example

Describe a sketch plan for y=x2−4x+3y=x^2-4x+3y=x2−4x+3.

  1. Mark roots 1 and 3 and stationary minimum (2,−1)(2,-1)(2,−1).
  2. Draw an upward-opening curve decreasing before x=2 and increasing after it.

Answer: A labelled upward parabola through the three key coordinates
