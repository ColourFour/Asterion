Skill 1 of 5

Complete the square and read quadratic form

Rewrite a quadratic so its vertex is visible.

Need to know

  • Factor the leading coefficient when it is not 1.
  • Balance the added square term with the constant outside the bracket.

Common error: Changing the sign of the bracket shift when reading the vertex.

Worked example

Write x2−6x+11x^2-6x+11x2−6x+11 in completed-square form.

  1. x2−6x=(x−3)2−9x^2-6x=(x-3)^2-9x2−6x=(x−3)2−9.
  2. Add 11 to obtain (x−3)2+2(x-3)^2+2(x−3)2+2.

Answer: (x−3)2+2(x-3)^2+2(x−3)2+2

Classify roots without solving the quadratic.

  • Use D>0D>0D>0, D=0D=0D=0 and D<0D<0D<0 for two, one repeated, or no real roots.

Common error: Using the sign of c instead of the sign of the discriminant.

Worked example

Classify the roots of 2x2+4x+2=02x^2+4x+2=02x2+4x+2=0.

Skill 3 of 5

Solve quadratic equations and inequalities

Solve quadratic equations and use their roots in sign intervals.

Need to know

  • Put all terms on one side before solving.
  • For an inequality, test the intervals separated by the roots.

Common error: Treating a quadratic inequality as if only the boundary roots were required.

Worked example

Solve x2−x−6<0x^2-x-6<0x2−x−6<0.

  1. Factor: (x−3)(x+2)<0(x-3)(x+2)<0(x−3)(x+2)<0.
  2. The upward-opening quadratic is negative between its roots.

Answer: −2<x<3-2<x<3−2<x<3

Skill 4 of 5

Solve linear-quadratic simultaneous equations

Reduce a linear-quadratic pair to one quadratic equation.

Need to know

  • Rearrange the linear equation for one variable.
  • Back-substitute every resulting root.

Common error: Finding x-values but omitting their paired y-values.

Worked example

Solve y=x+1y=x+1y=x+1 and x2+y=7x^2+y=7x2+y=7.

  1. Substitute to get x2+x−6=0x^2+x-6=0x2+x−6=0.
  2. So x=2x=2x=2 or x=−3x=-3x=−3, giving y=3y=3y=3 or y=−2y=-2y=−2.

Answer: (2,3)(2,3)(2,3) and (−3,−2)(-3,-2)(−3,−2)
