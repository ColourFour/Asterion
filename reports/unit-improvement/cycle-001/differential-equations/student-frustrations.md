# Student Frustration Report — Differential Equations — Cycle 1

## Student Persona
Reluctant student.
Does not especially like maths.
Only taking P3 because it is required.
Wants to know exactly what to do, why it matters, and how it helps with the exam.

## Frustrations
### 1. The first task still feels like a method jump
Location: Learn Mode / Recognize a separable equation

Impact: A reluctant student can try to pattern-match the answer without knowing what exam move the step is training.

Why this annoys the student: The first prompt says "Can this equation be separated?" after a stem about "You are given $\frac{dy}{dx}=xy$. You cannot integrate it as one ordinary expression in $x$ because $y$ is also chang...". I want the page to tell me the exact first action and why it is worth doing before I commit to algebra.

Suggested direction: Keep the problem-first setup, but make the first action and the exam reason explicit in the prompt.

### 2. The unit feels long before I know what is essential
Location: Learn Mode sequence

Impact: 12 checked steps can feel like a wall of tasks rather than a short route to exam readiness.

Why this annoys the student: I do not dislike every step, but I need to know which steps are core exam moves and which are support moves. Otherwise I feel trapped in a checklist.

Suggested direction: Use tighter step titles, exam-transfer copy, or deferred cleanup to show the shortest route through the unit.

## Confusion Points
### 1. Need-to-Know status mixes ready and draft skills
Location: Need-to-Know checklist

Impact: Students may assume every skill has equal review depth.

Why this annoys the student: This topic has 0 ready skill(s), 3 draft skill(s), 0 review-only skill(s), and 0 missing skill(s). I need the unit to be honest about what is fully ready.

Suggested direction: Keep draft/review-only labels visible and avoid using them as mastery proof.

## Boring / Low-Motivation Sections
### 1. Too much of the Skill Check can be answered by recognition
Location: Skill Check

Impact: 11/12 deterministic checks use multiple-choice or checkbox input.

Why this annoys the student: Multiple-choice is useful, but if most checks are selection tasks I can pass by recognizing the option instead of doing exam-like working.

Suggested direction: Future cycles should add typed deterministic variants where answer forms are stable.

## Missing Feedback
### 1. Common mistake warnings are sparse
Location: Field Guide examples

Impact: 0/4 examples include a visible common-mistake takeaway.

Why this annoys the student: I usually lose marks through predictable traps. I want the page to warn me before I make the mistake, not only after a wrong answer.

Suggested direction: Add targeted common-mistake lines where the mark-scheme trap is obvious and syllabus-safe.
