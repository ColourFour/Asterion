Absolutely. The target is not “make it prettier.” The target is:

Make the Asterion homepage clearly communicate what the product is, what is mature, where students should start, and why it feels serious.

Here are 12 concrete goals I’d use.

1. Reframe the homepage around the learning loop, not the course list

Current problem: the page says “pick a paper.” That is administratively correct but emotionally weak.

Goal: make the first thing students see:

Field Guide → Skill Check → Exam Training

That is the actual Asterion product. The course cards should come after the promise, not be the promise.

Success condition: within 5 seconds, a new visitor understands that Asterion is a guided CAIE 9709 training system, not just a list of notes.

2. Make P3 the primary path

Current problem: P3 is the most developed, but the homepage gives P1/P3/M1/S1 nearly equal weight.

Goal: visually promote P3 as the main complete experience.

Something like:

Start with P3
Most developed course. Full Field Guide, Skill Check, and Exam Training flow.

Then put P1/M1/S1 underneath as “draft support” or “early access.”

Success condition: nobody mistakes the draft courses for fully reviewed courses.

3. Add honest maturity labels to every course

This matters. Hiding draft status makes the project feel less trustworthy.

Possible labels:

P3 — Active / Most complete
P1 — Draft support
M1 — Draft support
S1 — Draft support

Do not over-explain. Just label clearly.

Success condition: the homepage tells the truth without apologizing.

4. Rewrite the hero copy to sound like a serious exam tool

Current copy is too soft. It feels like “nice study page.”

Goal: replace it with sharper copy.

Example direction:

CAIE 9709 practice that starts from the method, not the mark scheme.

Then:

Use the Field Guide to learn the route, answer a short Skill Check, then move into exam-style training.

Success condition: the page feels like it has a point of view.

5. Replace or remove the current hero illustration

The current illustration is not awful, but it is doing the wrong job. It makes the page feel younger and softer than the actual product.

Goal: either remove it entirely or replace it with a tighter “training flow” panel showing:

Field Guide
Skill Check
Exam Training
Guardian / Review

Success condition: the visual reinforces the product loop instead of decorating the page.

6. Add a “recommended next step” block

The homepage should reduce decision fatigue.

Example:

Recommended start
New here? Start with P3 Algebra Field Guide.

Or, for the multi-course page:

Best current path
Start with P3 Pure Mathematics 3. The other courses are available as draft support.

Success condition: a student does not have to think about where to click first.

7. Redesign course cards with hierarchy

Current course cards are too equal.

Goal: create one large featured P3 card, then three smaller draft cards.

Featured P3 card should include:

Field Guide
Skill Check
Exam Training
Best current experience

Draft cards should be simpler:

Topic guide
Skill checks
Draft support

Success condition: the layout communicates priority before the user reads the text.

8. Add topic-preview evidence to the homepage

Right now the homepage does not show enough of what makes Asterion useful.

Goal: show a tiny preview of real topics/methods.

Example:

Inside P3
Partial fractions
Binomial expansion
Vectors
Complex numbers
Differential equations

Even better: show one sample mini-flow:

Example path: Algebra → Partial Fractions → Field Guide → Skill Check

Success condition: the homepage feels connected to real student work, not abstract navigation.

9. Make the visual identity sharper

The current warm parchment style is fine for a study site, but Asterion should feel more like a focused exam-training map.

Goal: adjust the homepage style toward:

cleaner contrast
less decorative softness
stronger buttons
tighter spacing
more “system” energy
less “worksheet” energy

Success condition: it still feels approachable, but no longer feels cute or generic.

10. Add a short trust/status section

This should be small but important.

Example:

Current build status
P3 is the most developed course. P1, M1, and S1 are being expanded quickly and may contain draft material.

This protects you from overclaiming and actually makes the project look more serious.

Success condition: honesty becomes part of the brand.

11. Improve button language

Buttons should be action-oriented, not passive.

Instead of:

Open course

Use:

Start P3
Browse P1 draft
Open M1 support
Open S1 support

For internal topic pages:

Start Field Guide
Take Skill Check
Go to Exam Training

Success condition: every click tells the student exactly what they are doing.

12. Create a homepage acceptance checklist

Before calling the homepage done, test it against these questions:

Can a new student explain what Asterion does after 5 seconds?
Can they tell which course is most complete?
Do they know where to start?
Does the page feel like a serious product rather than a static folder?
Is the draft status honest?
Is the visual design helping, or just decorating?

Success condition: if the answer is not yes to all six, the page is not done.

My recommended build order:

First, rewrite the homepage structure and copy. Second, make P3 the featured course and demote the others into honest draft cards. Third, replace the hero illustration with a product-flow panel. Fourth, tighten the CSS. Do not start by polishing colors. That is the trap. The hierarchy is wrong first; styling comes after.
